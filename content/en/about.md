## section#story

- story.title: Our story
- story.paragraphs:
  - We've seen abaya tailor shops from the inside: an owner who knows her customers by name, a counter team who remember the measurements, and a receipt book full of numbers and notes. The work is excellent, but all the organisation lives on paper and in people's memory.
  - Much of the software we tried was either generic and didn't know what an armhole or a sleeve length from the neck is, or English first, or too complicated for someone standing in front of a waiting customer.
  - So we built Darjina to fit the shop: a screen that starts with a mobile number, measurements on the abaya picture itself, a receipt sent on WhatsApp, and payments that never go missing.

## section#mission

- mission.title: Our mission
- mission.text: To give every abaya shop in the GCC, whether small or with several branches, a simple Arabic tool that organises orders, measurements and money, so it can focus on what it does best: tailoring and looking after its customers.

## section#principles

- principles.title: What we stand by
- principles.items:
  - principles.item1.title: Arabic first
    principles.item1.text: Every screen was designed in Arabic and right-to-left, then translated into English, not the other way round.
  - principles.item2.title: Speed at the counter
    principles.item2.text: Your customer shouldn't wait long. Every step is designed to be done in seconds.
  - principles.item3.title: Money kept clear
    principles.item3.text: Every payment is recorded, and nothing is changed silently. A correction is a clear entry with a reason and a date.
  - principles.item4.title: Your shop's data stays your shop's
    principles.item4.text: Every shop is isolated from the others, and we don't sell or share data.
  - principles.item5.title: We only promise what we deliver
    principles.item5.text: What's available, we say is available; what's coming, we call "coming soon", without promising dates.
