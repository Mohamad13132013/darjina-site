Made for abaya shops, with people who know the trade

Darjina is an app from Bahrain that started with a simple question: why do most abaya shops still rely on a receipt book, when their customers are on WhatsApp all day?

Our story

We've seen abaya tailor shops from the inside: an owner who knows her customers by name, a counter team who remember the measurements, and a receipt book full of numbers and notes. The work is excellent, but all the organisation lives on paper and in people's memory.

Much of the software we tried was either generic and didn't know what an armhole or a sleeve length from the neck is, or English first, or too complicated for someone standing in front of a waiting customer.

So we built Darjina to fit the shop: a screen that starts with a mobile number, measurements on the abaya picture itself, a receipt sent on WhatsApp, and payments that never go missing.

Why "Darjina"?

Across the Middle East and Western Asia, a tailor is called darji, tarji or tarzi, words that all come from the same root. We chose Darjina as a feminine form of darji, because the app is made for tailoring traditional women's clothing.

Where we are today

Darjina is available now on iPhone through TestFlight, and the Android version is ready. We're starting in Bahrain, and the app is ready for the rest of the GCC, with each shop's own currency and country code.
